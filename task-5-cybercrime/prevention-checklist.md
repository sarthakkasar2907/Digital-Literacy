•	Always remember that you never need to enter your UPI PIN or scan a QR code to receive money. If someone tells you to enter a PIN to "verify" a payment coming into your account, they are trying to rob you.
•	For small daily expenses (canteen, tea, local transport), use UPI Lite or link a secondary bank account with a small balance to your payment apps. Keep your main savings account unlinked or on a separate device.
•	Use the "Lock Profile" feature on Facebook and set your Instagram to private. Scammers often scrape photos of college students to create "Deepfake" or "Sextortion" scams.
•	If you get a WhatsApp or Telegram message offering high pay for "liking YouTube videos" or "rating hotels," block them immediately. These are "Task Scams" designed to lure you into a Ponzi-style trap.
•	Avoid logging into your banking apps or sensitive student portals while on open, public, or hostel Wi-Fi. If you must use it, ensure you are using a reputable VPN to encrypt your data.
•	 Enable 2FA on your Gmail, Instagram, and LinkedIn. Use an authenticator app (like Google Authenticator) rather than just SMS, as "SIM Swapping" is a common tactic in india.
•	If you get a call from someone claiming to be "Police," "CBI," or "Customs" (the "Digital Arrest" scam), hang up. Genuine authorities will never contact you via WhatsApp video call or ask you to transfer money for "verification."
•	Never send sensitive or private photos/videos even if using "View Once" features. These can be easily captured via screen recording or another camera, and are frequently used in blackmail schemes.
•	Call the National Cyber Crime Helpline at 1930 immediately.
•	Register your case formally at the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal .
