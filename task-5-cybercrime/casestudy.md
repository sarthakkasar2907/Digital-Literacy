Upi/Online Payment Fraud has risen a lot in past few years due to introduction of cashless payment mode used frequently by all the people in the countries.
Upi fraud is not a very complex type of fraud as it does not involve anything related to the bank account.It is a fraud which is mostly done with mind games played by the scammer,
for example Rohit lists his laptop for ₹40000 on a popular second-hand marketplace.
Within ten minutes, he gets a call from a polite man claiming to be a business owner who needs the laptop for his office immediately.
How It Happens:-
Step 1: The buyer  doesn't haggle. In fact, he’s so eager he offers to pay a ₹10,000 advance right now to hold the item.
Step 2:- He sends Arjun a QR code via WhatsApp. Just scan this, he says. I’ve already authorized the payment from my business account.
Scanning it will credit the money to your bank.
Step 3:- In the rush of the moment, Arjun scans the code and sees a screen asking for his UPI pin. The scammer stays on the line,
chatting casually to keep Arjun distracted and prevent him from reading the fine print.
Step 4:- The moment Arjun enters his PIN, his phone buzzes—not with a credit notification, but an SMS stating that ₹10,000 has
been debited from his account. The QR code wasn't for receiving money; it was a "collect request."
Its targets are the online sellers on platforms like olx,facebook and small business owner who are used to fast upi 
transactions and might not check a payment failed claim from customers.
1) Instant Loss: Unlike credit card fraud, UPI transfers are real-time and irreversible. Once that PIN is entered,
  the money moves from person’s bank to a scammer account or a digital wallet in seconds.
2) Emotional Toll: There’s a deep sense of "tech-shame." Victims often feel foolish for falling for a trick that seems obvious in hindsight,
   which stops them from reporting it to the police or their bank.
